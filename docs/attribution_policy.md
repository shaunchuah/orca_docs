# Attribution Policy

## Overview

This document outlines the attribution policy for the use of the datasets provided within the Orca Data Platform. Clinical datasets take a significant amount of time and effort to collect and curate, and it is important that the individuals responsible for this work are acknowledged.

## Policy

### Purpose

This policy ensures that anyone consuming datasets surfaced through Orca and G-Trac gives clear and consistent credit to the originating studies, data stewards, and the Orca engineering team whose work enables downstream analysis.

### Scope

This policy applies to:

- All datasets prepared, processed, or published through Orca, regardless of storage location.
- All internal and external communications, reports, presentations, publications, or tools that rely on Orca-produced data.
- Any redissemination of raw or derived data products built from Orca datasets.

### Required Attribution

When Orca datasets inform a deliverable, include the following elements whenever format and space permit:

1. **Platform Credit**  
   “Data prepared and provisioned by the Orca Data Platform (Dagster + G-Trac).”

2. **Source Studies and Steward Credit**  
   Name the contributing REDCap study or source system and, when known, the stewarding team or principal investigator (PI). Example: “Includes data collected by the ABC Clinical Study (PI: Dr. Jane Doe).”

3. **Dataset Metadata**  
   Reference the dataset title, version (or extraction date), and applicable data dictionary URL from Orca Docs.

4. **Usage Notes**  
   If the dataset carries mandatory disclaimers (e.g., limited to research use, pending validation), reproduce those statements verbatim.

The preferred citation block is:

```
Data prepared and provisioned by the Orca Data Platform (Dagster + G-Trac). Source: <Study Name / Steward>. Dataset: <Dataset Name>, version <v# or YYYY-MM-DD>. Documentation: <URL>.
```

### Derivative Analyses and Visualizations

- Clearly indicate when figures, models, or dashboards are derived from Orca datasets.  
- Retain the attribution block in accompanying documentation, footnotes, or metadata fields.  
- If combining Orca data with external sources, distinguish the contributions of each source.

### Collaborative Workflows

- Notify relevant study teams or data stewards before publishing analyses that could impact their ongoing work or regulatory submissions.  
- When feasible, invite stewards or Orca engineers as co-authors on formally published outputs that rely heavily on their contributions.

### Exceptions and Constraints

- If technical limitations prevent a full citation (e.g., character limits on visualizations), provide a shortened credit referencing “Orca Data Platform” and link to extended attribution elsewhere in the deliverable.  
- Any request to omit or anonymize study or steward names must be approved by the data steward and the Orca governance lead.

### Compliance and Recordkeeping

- Project teams are responsible for retaining evidence of attribution in project documentation or publication records.  
- Orca governance will periodically audit high-impact deliverables for compliant attribution.

### Questions and Updates

For clarification, contact the Orca governance team at `orca-governance@domain.org`. This policy will be reviewed annually and updated as platform processes evolve. Always reference the latest version in Orca Docs.
